What does this strategy enable?

Mass spectrometry imaging–guided molecular networking connects where a metabolite occurs with how its fragmentation spectrum relates to other molecules. Spatially resolved MSI features are used to identify chemically informative regions or ions, while LC-MS/MS molecular networking supplies fragmentation-based family context and annotation. The combination can focus structural analysis on spatially localized natural products rather than treating imaging and molecular networking as independent readouts.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Acquire spatial metabolite maps

    Use MALDI or another suitable imaging-MS method to map metabolite ions across the tissue or material of interest.

  2. 2

    Prioritize spatially informative features

    Select ions or chemical regions whose localization suggests biological or anatomical relevance.

  3. 3

    Acquire complementary LC-MS/MS data

    Generate fragmentation-rich LC-MS/MS data from matched or relevant material so spatially prioritized chemistry can be interpreted structurally.

  4. 4

    Build molecular networks

    Organize related MS/MS spectra into molecular families and connect the spatially prioritized features to family-level structural context.

    GNPS
  5. 5

    Validate spatial and structural assignments

    Confirm prioritized annotations and localization with orthogonal standards, fragmentation interpretation, or additional imaging experiments where possible.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Natural-product or specialized-metabolite discovery where spatial localization can guide which chemistry deserves structural follow-up.
  • Multimodal studies combining imaging MS with fragmentation-rich LC-MS/MS.

Important requirements

  • Spatially resolved MSI data and complementary MS/MS data that can be related to the same chemistry.

Considerations

  • Matching an MSI ion to an LC-MS/MS feature requires careful mass, adduct, and sample-context reasoning.
  • Spatial colocalization and spectral family membership do not substitute for complete structural confirmation.
