3D molecular cartography

A spatial metabolomics strategy that combines systematic surface sampling, LC-MS/MS, coordinate-aware metadata and 2D/3D molecular mapping to reconstruct the distribution of metabolites and environmental chemicals across physical objects.

What does this strategy enable?

3D molecular cartography extends metabolomics beyond conventional bulk samples by attaching each extraction to a physical coordinate. LC-MS data are processed and annotated, then mapped onto photographs or 3D surface models so chemical distributions can be explored spatially. Cartographical snapshots preserve visualization settings for reproducibility.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Define a spatial sampling scheme

    Record coordinates for reproducible sampling across a surface, organism or object.

  2. 2

    Extract and profile each location

    Analyze spatially indexed extracts by LC-MS/MS with appropriate blanks and quality controls.

  3. 3

    Process and annotate features

    Generate a quantitative feature table and molecular annotations while preserving spatial sample identifiers.

  4. 4

    Map molecular features onto the physical model

    Join feature abundance to sampling coordinates and visualize the chemical distribution on a 2D image or 3D model.

  5. 5

    Share cartographical snapshots

    Preserve the mapped view and visualization settings so collaborators can reproduce the spatial interpretation.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Mapping extractable small molecules over complex surfaces at scales larger than pixel-based MSI.
  • Environmental, ecological, built-environment and organism-surface chemistry.

Important requirements

  • A reproducible coordinate system connecting each extraction to the mapped object.
  • Sufficient sampling density for the spatial question.

Considerations

  • Spatial resolution is determined by sampling geometry and is much coarser than many MSI methods.
  • Observed distributions integrate extraction, LC-MS detectability and sampling effects.
