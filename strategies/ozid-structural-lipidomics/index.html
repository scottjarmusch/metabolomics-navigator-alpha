Ozone-induced dissociation for structural lipidomics

A structural-lipidomics strategy that uses ozone-induced dissociation to generate diagnostic fragments for carbon–carbon double-bond positions that conventional CID-based lipidomics often cannot resolve.

What does this strategy enable?

Ozone-induced dissociation (OzID) adds gas-phase chemical reactivity to lipid analysis. Reactions of mass-selected unsaturated lipid ions produce diagnostic fragments that locate carbon–carbon double bonds. The method complements collision-induced fragmentation. Later implementations can combine separation and automated interpretation, but their acquisition and software requirements are implementation-specific.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Acquire lipids under OzID-compatible conditions

    Introduce ozone into a compatible MS workflow so unsaturated lipid ions undergo diagnostic gas-phase oxidation.

  2. 2

    Collect diagnostic OzID fragments

    Acquire fragment pairs that encode the location of carbon–carbon double bonds.

  3. 3

    Resolve and score double-bond assignments

    Interpret diagnostic fragment pairs; a later tool such as LipidOz can automate this for its supported OzID–ion mobility MS acquisition format.

    LipidOz ↗
  4. 4

    Validate structural isomers

    Confirm critical assignments with standards, retention/ion-mobility behavior or orthogonal structural evidence when available.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Resolving lipid double-bond positional isomers beyond conventional sum-composition or acyl-chain annotation.

Important requirements

  • Instrumentation or reaction setup capable of ozone-induced dissociation.
  • Appropriate acquisition methods and candidate lipid context.

Considerations

  • OzID adds structural specificity but does not automatically resolve every lipid stereochemical ambiguity.
  • Instrumentation and reaction implementation are platform-dependent.
  • The primary source introduces OzID. LipidOz is a later software implementation; OzFAD and other variants are not treated as interchangeable methods here.
