Suspect screening of exposome-related xenobiotics

An exposomics strategy that interrogates untargeted UHPLC-HRMS/MS data with curated lists of plausible xenobiotics and metabolites, then applies chromatographic, spectral, and error-control evidence to prioritize candidate exposures.

What does this strategy enable?

Suspect screening occupies the space between targeted analysis and fully non-target screening. Rather than requiring authentic standards for every analyte or searching every detected feature without prior hypotheses, the workflow begins with curated suspect lists and tests whether those candidates are supported by accurate mass, chromatographic peak quality, fragmentation evidence, and matrix-aware filtering. The 2024 workflow evaluated this approach across multiple human biofluids and explicitly measured false-positive and false-negative behavior.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Build suspect lists

    Assemble plausible parent xenobiotics and phase-II metabolites relevant to the exposure question and sample matrix.

  2. 2

    Acquire UHPLC-HRMS/MS data

    Analyze biofluids using high-resolution MS with fragmentation information suitable for candidate assessment.

  3. 3

    Filter chromatographic features for peak quality

    Apply automated peak-quality criteria to reduce poor chromatographic candidates before manual review.

  4. 4

    Compare candidates with suspect and endogenous lists

    Evaluate expected xenobiotic candidates while accounting for endogenous molecules that may create annotation interference.

  5. 5

    Evaluate structural evidence

    Combine accurate mass, isotope/adduct behavior, fragmentation evidence, and available reference information to annotate candidates.

  6. 6

    Estimate identification errors

    Assess false positives and false negatives using standards or spiked samples where available.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Screening broad but hypothesis-informed sets of environmental or exogenous chemicals in human biofluids.
  • Exposomics studies where fully targeted assays would be too narrow but unconstrained non-target identification is impractical.

Important requirements

  • Curated suspect lists appropriate to the study question.
  • High-resolution MS data and enough spectral/chromatographic evidence to evaluate candidates.

Considerations

  • Suspect-list composition determines which exposure space is visible.
  • Matrix interferences and endogenous molecules can create both false positives and false negatives.
