Native MS metabolomics for metal-binding compounds

A transferable LC-MS strategy that combines post-column pH adjustment and metal infusion with ion identity molecular networking to discover small molecules that bind metals in complex biological samples.

What does this strategy enable?

This strategy adapts native electrospray concepts to metabolomics so that protonated and metal-bound forms of the same chromatographic analyte can be detected together. Post-column pH adjustment and infusion of a selected metal are coupled to feature processing and ion identity molecular networking, allowing candidate metal-binding molecules to be prioritized from characteristic mass offsets and shared chromatographic behavior.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Separate metabolites by LC

    Analyze the extract using an LC-MS method suitable for retaining native-like metal-binding interactions after chromatographic separation.

  2. 2

    Adjust post-column pH

    Infuse a volatile base or buffer after the LC column to establish conditions compatible with observing metal complexes during electrospray.

  3. 3

    Infuse the metal of interest

    Introduce a metal salt post-column so protonated and metal-bound forms of candidate ligands can be observed with matched retention behavior.

  4. 4

    Process features and ion identities

    Detect and align chromatographic features and group coeluting ion species with correlated peak shapes and expected mass relationships.

    MZmine
  5. 5

    Build an ion identity molecular network

    Use ion identity molecular networking to connect ion species and spectral relationships, highlighting candidate metal-binding compounds.

    MZmineGNPSCytoscape ↗
  6. 6

    Prioritize and validate candidate binders

    Interpret expected metal-adduct mass offsets, inspect spectra and chromatograms, and validate prioritized compounds with standards or orthogonal evidence where available.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Discovering metal-binding small molecules in complex extracts using standard LC-MS hardware with post-column modification.
  • Comparing protonated and metal-bound ion species through chromatographic correlation and defined mass offsets.

Important requirements

  • LC-MS system with a post-column setup that permits pH adjustment and metal-salt infusion.
  • Appropriate blanks and controls to distinguish biologically relevant complexes from background or source-derived adducts.

Considerations

  • Metal identity, pH, salt concentration and electrospray conditions can alter complex formation and should be optimized and reported.
  • The network highlights candidate binding behavior; structural identity and biological relevance require additional evidence.
