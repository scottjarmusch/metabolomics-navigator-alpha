Chemoselective reactivity-resolved metabolomics

A functional-group-resolved metabolomics strategy that uses orthogonal chemoselective probes, covalent capture and diagnostic reporter chemistry to enrich and organize otherwise chemically heterogeneous metabolite subsets before LC-MS.

What does this strategy enable?

The modular reactivity-encoding platform (MREP) uses distinct alkyne-tagged probes for carboxyl, amine, carbonyl and thiol functionalities. Tagged metabolites are captured through click chemistry on a common resin, matrix is removed, and released derivatives share reporter-ion behavior. The strategy converts intrinsic chemical reactivity into an experimental annotation/enrichment dimension.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Select a functional-group-specific probe

    Choose a chemoselective probe for the metabolite reactivity class of interest, such as carboxyl, amine, carbonyl or thiol groups.

  2. 2

    Encode metabolites by covalent derivatization

    React the sample under conditions designed to selectively label the targeted functional group.

  3. 3

    Capture and clean up the encoded submetabolome

    Use click chemistry and solid-phase capture to enrich tagged metabolites while reducing matrix background.

  4. 4

    Release and analyze derivatives by LC-MS/MS

    Profile enriched derivatives, using shared reporter behavior and functional-group information to aid classification and annotation.

  5. 5

    Reconstruct reactivity-resolved metabolome layers

    Compare the parallel functional-group-defined measurements to expand detectable chemical space and interpret metabolic changes.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Expanding metabolite coverage through functional-group-selective enrichment and reporter chemistry.
  • Separating complex metabolomes into chemically interpretable reactivity classes.

Important requirements

  • Validated chemoselective reagents and controls for reaction selectivity.
  • Careful assessment of derivatization yield, matrix effects and quantitative behavior.

Considerations

  • Probe selectivity and conversion efficiency are chemistry-dependent.
  • Derivatization changes retention, ionization and fragmentation and therefore requires method-specific references.
  • The source demonstrates mouse serum and liver; transfer to other organisms and matrices requires method validation.
