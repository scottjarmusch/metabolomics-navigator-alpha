What does this strategy enable?

This strategy uses two complementary MALDI information spaces before scale-up. Protein-region spectra group bacterial isolates by taxonomic or strain-level similarity, while lower-mass specialized-metabolite spectra reveal chemical overlap within those groups. Isolates can then be selected to preserve phylogenetic breadth while minimizing redundant natural-product profiles, shifting strain prioritization from morphology or taxonomy alone to combined biological and chemical evidence.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Acquire MALDI protein fingerprints from individual isolates

    Analyze colony material in the higher mass range to generate replicate protein fingerprints suitable for similarity grouping.

    IDBac ↗
  2. 2

    Group taxonomically similar isolates

    Use protein-profile clustering to organize the collection into operational groups of closely related isolates.

    IDBac ↗
  3. 3

    Acquire specialized-metabolite profiles

    Analyze the lower mass region from the same isolate collection to capture differences in specialized-metabolite production.

    IDBac ↗
  4. 4

    Compare chemical overlap within taxonomic groups

    Use metabolite association patterns to identify isolates that add distinct chemistry rather than duplicating existing profiles.

    IDBac ↗
  5. 5

    Select a reduced but diverse library

    Prioritize representatives that retain taxonomic breadth and specialized-metabolite diversity for fermentation, extraction, and downstream discovery.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Building or reducing bacterial isolate libraries for natural-product discovery.
  • Prioritizing isolates before scale-up using both relatedness and chemical phenotype.

Important requirements

  • Cultured bacterial isolates and reproducible MALDI protein and small-molecule acquisitions.

Considerations

  • Culture conditions strongly influence specialized-metabolite profiles, so observed chemical redundancy is condition-dependent.
  • Protein spectral similarity is an operational grouping method and does not replace sequence-based taxonomy when definitive identification is required.
