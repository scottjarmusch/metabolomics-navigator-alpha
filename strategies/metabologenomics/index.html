Metabologenomics

A natural-product discovery strategy that correlates metabolite occurrence across a strain collection with biosynthetic gene-cluster or gene-cluster-family occurrence to connect chemistry with its likely genetic origin.

What does this strategy enable?

Metabologenomics treats a microbial strain collection as a panel of naturally varying experiments. Genome mining defines BGCs or GCFs, metabolomics defines spectra or molecular families, and co-occurrence across strains is scored to prioritize plausible BGC–metabolite links. The strategy can reveal both known and previously uncharacterized natural-product families without requiring a prior genetic hypothesis.

Transferable workflow

Workflow

  1. 1

    Profile a genetically diverse strain collection

    Generate comparable LC-MS/MS data across many sequenced isolates or conditions so metabolite occurrence can be compared across the collection.

  2. 2

    Mine genomes for biosynthetic gene clusters

    Identify BGCs and cluster related BGCs into gene-cluster families using genome-mining tools.

    antiSMASH ↗BiG-SCAPE ↗
  3. 3

    Organize metabolite features or molecular families

    Use molecular networking or related spectral grouping to define recurring chemical features across strains.

    GNPS
  4. 4

    Score BGC–metabolite co-occurrence

    Correlate the presence/abundance of metabolite features or molecular families with BGC/GCF occurrence across strains.

    NPLinker ↗
  5. 5

    Prioritize and validate links

    Use genetics, targeted cultivation, isolation, MS/NMR or heterologous expression to validate high-priority BGC–metabolite associations.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Linking specialized-metabolite chemistry to candidate biosynthetic gene clusters across strain collections.
  • Prioritizing biosynthetic hypotheses before direct genetic validation.

Important requirements

  • A sufficiently diverse, sequenced strain collection with comparable metabolomics data.
  • Consistent identifiers linking each genome/isolate to its metabolomics samples.

Considerations

  • Co-occurrence supports association, not causation; population structure and phylogeny can confound links.
  • Silent BGCs and condition-dependent metabolite production create false negatives.
  • The linked contemporary resources illustrate ways to implement the strategy; they are not all software used in the original publication.
