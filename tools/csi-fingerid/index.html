Structure database search with CSI:FingerID and COSMIC

CSI:FingerID predicts molecular fingerprints from fragmentation-tree information and uses them to rank candidate structures in molecular databases. The COSMIC workflow adds confidence estimation to help distinguish particularly trustworthy structure-database annotations from ambiguous candidate lists.

  1. Establish candidate molecular formulas

    Run SIRIUS formula annotation and retain plausible precursor formulas for structure analysis.

  2. Predict molecular fingerprints with CSI:FingerID

    Use fragmentation-tree-derived information and machine learning to predict structural fingerprint properties from the MS/MS spectrum.

  3. Search molecular-structure databases

    Rank database candidates whose structural fingerprints agree with the predicted fingerprint and molecular formula.

  4. Apply COSMIC confidence scoring

    Evaluate CSI:FingerID structure candidates with COSMIC to identify predictions that meet stronger confidence criteria and to separate high-confidence from ambiguous assignments.

  5. Validate prioritized structures

    Review candidate structures and corroborate important annotations with spectral standards, retention behavior, orthogonal data, or other appropriate evidence.

Requirements

  • A plausible molecular formula and informative high-resolution MS/MS spectrum; CSI:FingerID/COSMIC are webservice-backed SIRIUS features requiring appropriate account/license access.

Considerations

  • The method is constrained by the candidate structure databases being searched; compounds absent from those databases cannot be returned as exact database candidates.
  • A high computational confidence score should still be reported as an annotation unless the identification level is supported by appropriate orthogonal evidence.

Hoffmann MA, Nothias L-F, Ludwig M, et al. High-confidence structural annotation of metabolites absent from spectral libraries. Nature Biotechnology 40, 411–421 (2022).

Overview

CSI:FingerID predicts structural fingerprints from fragmentation-tree information and uses those fingerprints to search and rank candidate molecular structures in databases. In current SIRIUS releases it is a webservice-backed component of the structure-database search workflow rather than a standalone end-user application. It does not resolve stereochemistry and should be interpreted as ranked computational structure evidence rather than definitive identification.

What can this tool do?

Structure-database searchingSubstructure predictionStructure elucidation

Common uses

  • Rank molecular-structure database candidates from accurate-mass MS/MS spectra.
  • Predict molecular fingerprints that summarize structural properties inferred from fragmentation data.
  • Provide structure-candidate rankings used by the SIRIUS COSMIC workflow and downstream interpretation.
  • Prioritize database candidates for follow-up confirmation with standards or orthogonal measurements.

Scope and considerations

Designed for

  • Database-backed candidate structure identification of small molecules from informative, high-accuracy MS/MS spectra.

Not designed for

  • Unrestricted de novo generation of structures absent from the searched candidate database.

Important requirements

  • SIRIUS-compatible high-resolution MS/MS data, a SIRIUS account/license for the webservice, and a molecular formula or formula candidates.

Considerations

  • CSI:FingerID does not determine stereochemistry and cannot identify structures absent from the searched databases.
  • Ranking accuracy depends on spectral information content, molecular-formula quality, and candidate-database composition.
  • Record the candidate database and formula settings, and inspect competing structures before interpreting the highest-ranked hit.
